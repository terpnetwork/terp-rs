# Headstash 2026: team brief

We're proposing three Terp governance proposals that launch Headstash 2026, a private TERP airdrop to holders from 15 EVM communities plus the Solana community stoned-ape-club. 17,791 eligible identities share 66,456,669 TERP from the community pool, split into 98,841 notes. Proposal A deploys the airdrop contract and an on-chain, hash-verified claim page without funding them. Proposal B1 stores the zero-knowledge note circuit. Proposal B2 binds the circuit, opens the campaign and funds the contract. To claim, holders first register by signing a message with the wallet that held the NFT. They then claim with a zero-knowledge proof, so their Terp address isn't linked on-chain to that wallet. Claim fees are covered by fee grants funded by the foundation DAO, so claimers need no tokens. Governance (x/gov) is the admin of both contracts, and WAVS is disabled. Claims close at block 44,444,444. Before then, governance can reclaim the whole balance to the community pool in an emergency. After it, anything left unclaimed can only be burned. Penumbra-based privacy after the claim is planned but not live yet.

Order and signers: terp1gnfq (the dabarber validator key) proposes using Zigner together with a short-lived session key. Each proposal needs a 10,000 TERP deposit (5,000 at submission plus a 5,000 top-up). That wallet can only fund one proposal at a time, so the proposals run one after the other: A first (after it passes, terp1fx grants the small capped claim-page upload allowance, the page chunks are uploaded and sealed, and the grant is revoked), then B1, then B2, each after the previous one passes and its deposit is refunded (or a second depositor adds the top-up). After B2 passes, terp1fx issues the claimer fee grants. terp1fx first needs a top-up of about 2,572 THIOL. Still pending before anything is signed: (1) host the three metadata files in `tools/headstash/deploy/proposal-metadata/` (e.g. on IPFS), confirm the hosted copies match `SHA256SUMS`, and fill in their URIs; (2) regenerate each of the two session grants (one for the Phase A stores, one for the B1 circuit proposal) at signing time; (3) check on the Zigner device that each sign document renders correctly. The step-by-step checklist is `tools/headstash/deploy/OPERATOR-CHECKLIST.md`. The discussion is at https://discord.terp.network.
